# The Telegram tap, built for throughput

## Summary

In the target tier a button tap on an approval card is admitted, deduplicated on its `update_id`, answered with a 200 — and dropped. No handler consumes the `v1:<action>:<intent>` buttons the cards carry, nothing answers the tap, nothing flips the intent — and the registered webhook does not even ask Telegram for taps (`allowed_updates` is `message` only), so a handler alone would ship dead. The plan's W4 inbound half (`01` §1, `02` §5 "interactive commands … executed inline in ingress") was never built; #1040 names it, #854 tracks the typed-command half. This plan builds the tap and, behind it, the two layers that decide whether the product survives thousands of taps at once: the API's admission and process shape, and the worker's parallelism, pacing and retry ceilings. The owner's requirement (2026-09-09): thousands of concurrent taps across many workspaces without a crash or a hang; on one card, rapid taps process the first and never hang; taps across many cards register at once and run in parallel where safe or wait where they must.

Phase 1 alone satisfies "build for 2 teams today" (`PROJECT_MISSION.md:36`): the buttons work, once, and answer. Phases 2–3 are the "design for 200" spend the owner asked for — proof and headroom, not the wedge — so a stalled phase 2 is not a failed epic. The wedge itself, a tap that *posts*, closes only when #1220 step 3 lands the publish leg; fork F11 decides where that leg sits in the order.

Hardening: ironclad cycle 1 (2026-09-09, nine lenses) is folded into this body. Its two blockers — taps never delivered, and a worker pool arithmetic that could not close — are resolved in phase 1's Dependencies and in F7.

## Architecture

**Where the tap lives.** The tap is a *command*, not a job: `TelegramDispatcher` grows a callback branch that resolves the chat to its workspace through `fn_resolve_binding`, the tapper to a user through `user_identities`, and hands a `Command(kind, workspace_id, actor_user_id, channel="telegram")` to `commands.execute` — the same path the web queue takes (`src/api/routes/v1.py:210` via `commands.ingest`). The webhook's existing admission on `update_id` stays the idempotency ledger; the port is not admitted twice (#985). Registration is part of the surface: `allowed_updates` names `callback_query`, and `max_connections` is set to the ingress connection budget (workers × pool) so Telegram's delivery concurrency and the pool are the same number.

**The one-transaction flip, made safe under repeat and race.** The intent executors read the row `FOR UPDATE` and decide *before* writing: a row in any state other than `awaiting_approval` answers with its current state (an `answered` outcome carrying `state`, `by`, `at`) and writes nothing — `review_required` included, because `review_required → approved` is the operator's edge and a member's tap must never take it; only the `awaiting_approval → <action>` edge writes. The database guard (055) and 061's self-transition guard stay the last line, not the first. Inside the transaction: the flip, the `post_locks` row, the `publish_pipeline` job when the action is `post`, the outbox rows that supersede every live card for the intent across every binding of the workspace, and — once S.2 applies to taps (F12) — the `ws_admission` debit, for an executed flip only. Nothing external runs inside it.

**Answering, and the one answer.** Telegram accepts one `answerCallbackQuery` per query, and the spinner it drives dies by itself at ~30 s (#557). So the answer is bound to a *budget*, not to the commit: when the flip ran within the ingress budget, the answer after commit carries the outcome text — executed, answered-by-state, or refused by name, with `show_alert=True` when the tapper must act; when a connection cannot be had within the budget, the route answers by name without a database ("Busy — tap again", F1 (d)) and returns 200 without admission — a tap is re-derivable because its buttons remain, and a spinner that dies unanswered is the hang the owner forbids. Beside the answer, an immediate, unpaced, best-effort `editMessageReplyMarkup(empty)` on the tapped message (its ref is the callback's own `message`, so the known-ref rule holds) removes the buttons in the tapping chat at once. The durable outcome — the original header plus an outcome line — rides the outbox as `prompt_supersede`, paced per chat, in every binding. Neither the answer nor the strip goes through the outbox: they are not messages but the client's spinner and a cosmetic edit, worthless late and harmless lost; pacing them behind the 2 s poller and the per-chat limit would re-create #557. This is where the plan departs from #1040's "the router never speaks Telegram outbound", by ruling.

**Cards end in every terminal state.** A card is retired by whatever settles its intent, not only by a tap: the reaper's expiry, `cancel`, `disable_account`, the pipeline's `posted` write and a tap all call `supersede_all(outcome_text=…)`; a sender whose card was superseded while in flight enqueues the supersede for the ref it just received; `_settle` enqueues the supersede for a stale tapped card so it heals on first touch.

**Under load.** The route's one timeout boundary is the pool's own: an ingress-specific pool timeout mapped to a refusal *before* admission (never `asyncio.wait_for` around a checkout, which can cancel mid-checkout and leak a connection). After admission only database errors around commit map to 503 (the admission row rolls back with them and Telegram redelivers once the fault clears); every other exception is caught in `_tap` and committed as outcome `tap_failed`, logged with `update_id`, alarmed — a poisoned update must not redeliver forever. Per-workspace admission (S.2) is checked before `admit()` and debited only for executed flips; its number for taps is fork F12. The process shape (F5) is an outcome of the harness, measured first at today's one process. Whether a tap is ever queued to the worker (F1) is decided by the harness on end-to-end tap→answer latency including redelivery and on the pre-admission refusal count — not on post-admission 5xx alone.

**The worker.** K concurrent claim-and-run tasks per lane in one process with **no pinned claim connections** — `jobs.claim_job` commits its own claim on a pooled checkout returned at once, and job sessions are released across holds and provider waits (transaction-per-checkpoint), so K bounds tasks, not connections, and Σ(replica × (pool + overflow)) = 50 stands (`05:41`). The sender commits the pacing debit and the claim before the provider call and marks after — in phase 1, because the tap's supersede needs the claim committed (F8). A 429 becomes a durable hold on the `tg_global` `rate_counters` row plus a rescheduled sender job, never an in-task sleep. Per-lane attempt and deadline budgets end retries with a terminal `failed`; the per-workspace cap rises to the `05` numbers; and ready depth, oldest-runnable age, the `tg_global`-paced count and the per-workspace oldest wait go into the status line so overload — and starvation, F9's trigger — is visible. Phase 3 lands as two PRs: 3a the S-sized fixes, 3b the K concurrency. Tenant fairness beyond the per-workspace cap is phase 4, flagged and not built until the harness or the status line shows starvation.

## Decision Forks

### Fork F1 — What happens to a tap when the API is saturated

Context: `02:1219` rules interactive commands inline (the ack is the transaction); R5 allows the work to continue async; the owner asks that taps "register and wait to process or process in parallel". Today saturation is a 3 s pool wait then a 500. The legacy tier's two live incidents (#557 open, #686 closed — "answer the callback FIRST") are the precedent: a tap refused before admission is unanswered until Telegram redelivers, the spinner dies at ~30 s, and `answerCallbackQuery` then rejects the stale query — a hang, invisible to a "5xx after admission" pass bar. Telegram delivers through at most `max_connections` simultaneous connections (default 40, max 100; `register` sets none), so under load the backlog forms at Telegram as `pending_update_count`, not at the route. #1040 asks that the router never speak Telegram outbound; the answer and the immediate strip bypass the outbox on purpose (Architecture, "Answering").
Options: (a) **Inline, always; 503 before admission.** Size the pool and processes, keep the transaction tiny, refuse before admission under a short budget so Telegram redelivers; no queue. (b) **Inline when cheap, queued under pressure.** Same as (a), plus a new `telegram_update` job kind (migration 072: `ck_jobs_kind` edit) the route falls back to when the pool wait exceeds the budget, answering the tap "Got it" at once; the worker executes the same command path. (c) **Always queued.** The route admits and enqueues; the worker flips and answers — it inherits phase 3's retries and `ready=` signal, sacrifices `02:1219`, costs 072, and the answer waits on claim latency (`work_loop.py:70` idles 1 s → p95 near H2's 2 s). (d) **Answer by name without a database.** After the parse and before any database work, when a connection cannot be had within the ingress budget the route answers "Busy — tap again" and returns 200 without admission: no queue, no redelivery, no spinner past the budget; the tap is re-derivable because its buttons remain. Its stronger form answers *every* tap "Got it" after the parse and carries the outcome only in the card edit (the #686 shape) — guaranteed sub-budget answers at the cost of the outcome toast.
Lean: (a) for the flip with (d)'s saturation answer in place of the 503 — inline, tiny, answered within budget whether or not it ran. Proven by the phase 2 harness on end-to-end tap→answer latency including redelivery (p95 < 2 s), zero 5xx, and a pre-admission refusal count reported and bounded; if the "Busy" rate or p95 misses at the `05` arithmetic, (b) is the named fallback and (d)'s stronger form the cheaper alternative — the fork is re-served with the numbers.
Ratifier: owner. Status: **locked** (owner, 2026-09-09 — "go with the plan; if the behaviour or experience is suboptimal we address it then"): (a) with (d) — the flip is inline and tiny; under saturation the tap is answered "Busy — tap again" and not admitted, never a 503; (b) stays the named fallback if the phase 2 report misses.

### Fork F2 — How a repeated tap is recognised

Context: Telegram issues a fresh `update_id` per tap, so `command_dedup` cannot collapse two taps on one card. Today the second tap errors because of 061's `tg_intent_no_self_transition` (`061:85-87`; #883/#886: under READ COMMITTED both writers were told they won) — the right layer sits over that guard, and phase 1's gate test (2) is its regression pin. `mark_posted` already decides and writes in one statement (`command_executors.py:237`, `:250-257`).
Options: (a) **Read-then-decide under a row lock.** `SELECT … FOR UPDATE` on the intent; a row in any state other than `awaiting_approval` answers with its current state and writes nothing. (b) **A per-(intent, action) dedup row** in `command_dedup` keyed on the intent rather than the update. (c) Both. (d) **Compare-and-swap.** `UPDATE post_intents SET state = :to WHERE id = :i AND state = 'awaiting_approval' RETURNING id` — decide and write in one statement, no `FOR UPDATE`, the trigger still the backstop; a miss re-reads the committed row to answer.
Lean: (a) — the executors already read the row before writing (`_refuse_if_cancelling`, `api_publishing_enabled`, the account ref the job needs), so the lock makes that read the decision at no extra statement; (d) is the fallback if `double_tap_one_card` shows the lock convoy in its p95 or 5xx count; (b) adds a second ledger for a question the row already answers.
Ratifier: owner. Status: **locked** (owner, 2026-09-09 — "go with the plan; if the behaviour or experience is suboptimal we address it then"): (a) — the first tap applied wins; a row past `awaiting_approval` answers with its state and writes nothing; Telegram arbitrates nothing, the app does; (d) is the fallback if the double-tap scenario shows the lock convoy.

### Fork F3 — Who a tap is attributed to

Context: the audit trigger refuses an anonymous state change; `06`'s join path makes only *linked* Telegram users members. X.3 ships the Telegram `link` start-token (D35, `04:344`), so a link is one tap away and need not send a teammate to the web mid-approval. In a group chat the name an answer shows is a disclosure choice.
Options: (a) **The tapper, resolved through `user_identities`**; an unlinked tapper is answered by name *with the link* — the answer (`show_alert=True`) carries the `link` start URL, or a one-button link card is pushed to the tapper's DM through the outbox — and nothing flips. (b) **The binding's admin** when the tapper is unknown. (c) Any tapper, recorded by Telegram id only.
Lean: (a) — attribution is the audit row's whole value; (b) forges it; (c) has no `user_id` for `created_by_user_id` on the lock rows. The name a card and an answer show (`settled_by`) is the member's workspace display name — the one the web queue shows — never an email-derived string in a shared chat.
Ratifier: owner. Status: **locked** (owner, 2026-09-09 — "go with the plan; if the behaviour or experience is suboptimal we address it then"): (a) — the tapper, through `user_identities`; an unlinked tapper is answered with the link and nothing flips; `settled_by` shows the workspace display name.

### Fork F4 — What the card shows after a tap

Context: the legacy product stripped the keyboard first and then edited the caption (`telegram_callbacks_core.py:66-80`); the target tier's only edit path is supersede-then-send (`outbox.supersede_all`, `outbox.py:366-403`), which has no caller and whose `prompt_supersede` rows the sender cannot render today (`telegram_transport.py:369-373`). The row does not know whether it was sent as a photo or as text (`outbox.py:310-314`; decided at `telegram_transport.py:351-376`), so `editMessageCaption` on a text fallback 400s into `failed`. The header (`📸 @handle` + slot, `prompts.py:139`) is what four accounts' cards differ by (`06:48`). Button removal that rides paced delivery (2 s poller, 18 sends/60 s per chat) leaves the last of 20 taps in one chat live for ~a minute, inviting re-taps.
Options: (a) **Edit in place, strip first.** The sender's `prompt_supersede` branch calls `editMessageReplyMarkup(empty)` first (type-agnostic — the call that must land), then best-effort `editMessageCaption` or `editMessageText` by the `sent_as` the row recorded at `mark_sent`, writing the original header plus the outcome line; and, from the route, an immediate unpaced strip on the tapped message beside the answer. (b) **A new message** under the old card, buttons left inert (a late tap answers per R6). (c) Delete the card and post the outcome. (d) **`editMessageReplyMarkup` alone**: the keyboard replaced with one inert outcome button (`✅ Approved by Chris · 2026-09-09 14:14`) — identical for photo and text cards, no caption edit, no `sent_as`; a tap on the inert button answers per R6. About 80 % of (a) at a third of the surface.
Lean: (a), with (d)'s call as its mandatory first step — the strip is what must land; the header-plus-outcome-line edit is best-effort and may fail without failing the row. (d) is the cut if the owner prefers one call and an outcome that lives in the button. Neither the answer nor the immediate strip goes through the outbox (F1 Context).
Ratifier: owner. Status: **locked** (owner, 2026-09-09 — "go with the plan; if the behaviour or experience is suboptimal we address it then"): (a) — edit in place, strip first (`editMessageReplyMarkup(empty)` is the call that must land), header plus outcome line best-effort by `sent_as`; an immediate unpaced strip from the route beside the answer.

### Fork F5 — The API's process shape

Context: one process today (`Procfile:2`, no `--workers`), `POOL_SIZE_SEAM = 10` per process (`unit_of_work.py:89`), overflow 0; Σ = 50 counts "2×10 ingress" (`05:52`). `railway.toml` sets no `startCommand`, so a Procfile edit takes effect. Telegram delivers at most `max_connections` updates at once, so the route's true concurrency is that number, not the harness's offered burst. The current shape has never been measured.
Options: (a) **`uvicorn --workers 2`** with `POOL_SIZE_SEAM = 10` per process on ONE replica — `05`'s `2 × 10` ingress connections, with `max_connections = 20` to match. (b) One process with a pool of 20. (c) More Railway replicas of one process each (two replicas × one worker × 10 is the same `2 × 10`; two replicas × two workers would be 40 and break Σ = 50 — the ruling must say which).
Lean: an outcome of the phase 2 report — the harness runs first at today's one process; (a) only if that baseline misses p95 with the "Busy" rate bounded. (a) over (b) for CPU parallelism on JSON and TLS with the budget unchanged; (c) costs money before it is shown to be needed.
Ratifier: owner. Status: **locked** (owner, 2026-09-09 — "go with the plan; if the behaviour or experience is suboptimal we address it then"): an outcome of the report — baseline at one process; (a) `--workers 2` on one replica with `max_connections = 20` only if the baseline misses p95.

### Fork F6 — Where the attempt ceiling is enforced

Context: `jobs.reschedule_job` never reads `max_attempts` (`jobs.py:302-336`); `_run_job` backs off a flat 60 s on any exception (`work_loop.py:644-661`); only `publish_pipeline` enforces attempts (`publish_pipeline.py:513`); `deadline_at` (`056:102`) is never read. `05:38` gives per-lane budgets — interactive 3 attempts, deadline +10 min; bulk 5 attempts, backoff 1/5/15/60 min, deadline = slot end (or +6 h for non-slot jobs) — and names no jitter. `deliver_outbox` (interactive, `max_attempts 3`) is re-minted by the sweep while rows are pending (`work_loop.py:707`, `:713-715`), so a "failed and will not retry" notice for it would be false.

### Fork F7 — Worker parallelism

Context: one `WorkLoop` per lane, one pinned claim connection each (`worker.py:413-415`), one job at a time per lane. The first draft pinned K claim connections and asserted `K_interactive + K_bulk + 2 ≤ POOL_SIZE_SEAM` — `8 + 4 + 2 = 14 > 10` at its own defaults — and pinned slots would have starved the job sessions, poller ticks and heartbeat checkouts that share the pool (Evidence), expiring leases and making the reaper re-ready running work. `05:41` rules a pool slot a task, not a connection; `jobs.claim_job` commits its own claim (`jobs.py:118-151`). The legacy twin is #713/#573 (`concurrent_updates(8)` as "the dominant multiplier on peak concurrent DB connections", `src/config/settings.py:222`). `05:31`'s 10:50 is the ceiling shape; 8:4 inverted it.
Options: (a) **K tasks per lane in one process, no pinned connections.** Each task claims through `jobs.claim_job` on a pooled checkout returned at once, runs the job with its session released across holds and provider waits (transaction-per-checkpoint, `02:1254`), and holds at most one connection, only inside a transaction. K is sized from the *measured* peak connections per running job: the 3b gate seeds K jobs of the most database-active kind (`deliver_outbox` under a slow fake) and asserts pool peak + election + clock + heartbeat ≤ `POOL_SIZE_SEAM` with zero `TimeoutError`; start at K = 4 interactive / 2 bulk (fits even if every task were DB-active at once: 4 + 2 + 3 ≤ 10) and raise on the measurement toward `05:31`'s 10/50. Σ = 50 stands. (b) More replicas, one job per lane each. (c) Both.
Lean: (a) — H6 says any replica runs any workspace's job, so replicas remain the scale-out lever, but a replica that runs two jobs wastes the pool it already holds.
Ratifier: owner. Status: **locked** (owner, 2026-09-09 — "go with the plan; if the behaviour or experience is suboptimal we address it then"): (a) — K tasks per lane with no pinned connections, K from the 3b measurement starting at 4 interactive / 2 bulk, Σ = 50 kept.

## What NOT To Do

- Do not process the tap in the request *before* admission, and do not answer 200 for an update whose command was neither executed, queued, nor answered by name — that loses the tap.
- Do not `asyncio.wait_for` a pool checkout; the pool's own timeout is the boundary.
- Do not raise out of `_tap` after admission for anything but a database error around commit; a poisoned update redelivers forever.
- Do not pin claim connections; do not hold a job session across a hold or a provider call.
- Do not sleep in-task on a 429 or pace in process memory (#1035).
- Do not introduce a generic `run_command` job kind (`02:1219`).
- Do not add a global command ceiling (D22).
- Do not edit a card on an ambiguous message ref; supersede only known refs.
- Do not build fairness machinery before the harness or the status line shows starvation (`04:350`).
- Do not assert timing with a wall-clock in a concurrency gate (#672).
