## Summary

Instagram refuses a freshly uploaded frame's first fetch about a quarter of the time, remembers the refusal per url for a minute or two, and once answered "resource does not exist" about a container it had reported ready four seconds earlier (`02_the-fetch-path_2026-09-13.md`). None of it is the file, the url type, the hour or our region, and all of it clears within minutes. The target state, ruled by the owner on 2026-09-14: **an approved story floats** — it tries, steps back, waits progressively longer, tries again, and the workspace sees "Approved" until it sees "Posted". A floating story holds nothing, so any number of them sit side by side and a burst drains at the speed of the successes. The workspace hears only if Instagram has refused for about forty minutes straight.

Read with `00_INVESTIGATION.md`, `01_first-fetch-and-failure-reporting.md`, and `02_the-fetch-path_2026-09-13.md` (the evidence this designs around).

## Target behaviour

For one approved story:

1. **Try.** Upload the frame, read its first bytes back (the readiness probe), ask Instagram for a container.
2. **Refused fetch → a fresh url at once.** Ask again within seconds with a different url to the same original (a separately derived, byte-identical frame), up to three fresh urls per story. Five of six such retries were accepted immediately in the experiments.
3. **Still refused → step back and wait.** Leave the account's publish slot, keep the progress and the cap debit, wait 30 s, then 60, 120, 300, 600, 900, retrying after each wait — about 40 minutes of patient trying in all.
4. **Container Instagram cannot find → recreate it.** On a publish-time `24/2207006`, drop the container id, step back, wait 10 s, then 30, 60, and create a new container from the same upload. Never re-publish an id Instagram says it cannot find.
5. **Posted.** Clear the story's error; the card reads "✅ Posted".
6. **Only then, the review card.** If the fetch ladder is spent (six waits) or the container ladder is spent (three), the story parks `review_required`: the card gains "👀 Needs review" and its three resolutions, and the workspace's channel gets one notice that says how long Instagram was tried ("Instagram refused this story for 41 minutes…"). "Post again" starts a fresh float.

What the workspace sees during 1–5: the card's "✅ Approved" line, nothing else. No notice for a retry in progress (already the rule).

Many at once: a waiting story is an `approved` row with its job due in the future. It holds no slot. Twenty stories float side by side; each steps back into the slot when its wait ends, and if a sibling is mid-call at that moment it re-checks 20 s later (`FlipOutcome.BUSY`, #1301).

## What does not float — unchanged

- **A lost publish answer.** Publishing is the irreversible step; the reconciler asks Instagram what happened before anything moves (`02` §6, R8). Never retried blind.
- **A definitive rejection of the file** (a terminal code other than 9004): fails and refunds, as today.
- **Meta's error 9 (the publish cap)** keeps today's behaviour: defer to the next product slot, holding the slot — every sibling would meet the same cap. Named here so the "waiting never holds the slot" rule is stated with its one exception; revisit if it ever bites.
- **A workspace pause, a cancel, a dry run**: as today.

## Design

### D1 — Fresh urls: `transit.delivery_url(ref, media_kind=, variant=k)`

`URL_VARIANTS = ([{"dpr": "1.0"}], [{"dpr": "1.0"}, {"dpr": "1.0"}], [{"dpr": "1.0"}] * 3)`; variant `k` (1–3) appends `URL_VARIANTS[k-1]` to the story chain — a no-op step Cloudinary validated in the experiments (`dpr_1.0`), so each variant is a distinct signed url and a distinct derived asset with identical bytes; variant 0 is today's url. `MAX_URL_VARIANTS = 3`. `ready()` takes `variant=` and probes the variant url (a first request derives it on the fly, 2–3 s, inside the probe's budget). Derived variants die with the original at destroy time (FC-3.5/3.6 unchanged).

### D2 — Counters per failure class on the story: `post_intents.attempts_by_step`

The column exists (`{"v": 1}`, unused; `02` §4). It becomes `{"v": 1, "url_variant": k, "fetch_refusals": n, "fetch_waits": w, "container_gone": m}`, loaded onto the pipeline context, bumped in the same transaction as the permit resolution that records the failure. Ladders index by the class's own count, never by the job's cumulative attempts:

| Class | Immediate retries | Waits (s) | Spent ⇒ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fetch refused (`9004/2207052`) | fresh url ×3 | 30, 60, 120, 300, 600, 900 | review card |
| Container gone (`24/2207006`) | — | 10, 30, 60 (recreate) | review card |
| Other retryable Meta answers | — | 60, 300, 900, 3600 (unchanged) | review card |

A fetch or container wait reschedules the job with the attempt RESTORED (`reschedule_job(restore_attempt=True)`): the job's five attempts stay the bound for the other classes and for crashes; the float is bounded by its own ladders. `_retry_or_poison` gains `attempt=` (which count indexes the rung) and `spent=` (whether that class's ladder is exhausted, which is what poisons).

### D3 — Step back: the new legal edge `publishing → approved`

- Migration `076_publish_wait_edge.sql`: `INSERT INTO post_intent_transitions (from_state, to_state) VALUES ('publishing', 'approved')` (a seed row, no DDL; the guard trigger reads the table). The audit row for the hop carries `detail = {"wait": "<class>", "seconds": n}`.
- `_retry_or_poison`, on a fetch or container wait: `UPDATE post_intents SET state = 'approved' WHERE id = :intent AND state = 'publishing'` in the same transaction as the permit resolution, the counter bump and the reschedule. `publish_step`, `transit_asset_ref`, `cap_consumed_on` are untouched — the progress and the debit stay. (Today's ladders leave the story `publishing`; that is what held the slot for seven siblings.)
- `publish_cap.flip_to_publishing` becomes re-entrant: the `debit` CTE runs only when `cap_consumed_on IS NULL`, and the `flip` CTE succeeds when a debit happened OR the story already carries one. Key 4 is still acquired by the flip, so a waiting story stepping back in while a sibling is mid-call gets `BUSY` (#1301) and re-checks in 20 s.
- The ladder resumes from the stored `publish_step` after the flip (`run_publish_pipeline` already does), so a re-entered story continues at `transit_uploaded` (probe → permit → container) or wherever it left off. A container-gone wait rewinds `publish_step` to `transit_uploaded` and nulls `ig_container_id` (the existing `step_back_to` path).
- The reaper expires `approved` rows after `approved_ttl_seconds` (72 h) — far beyond a 40-minute float — and `entered_state_at` restarts at each re-entry, so a floating story is never expired by it.
- **Cancel while floating.** `cancel` sets `cancel_requested`; `_admit` honours it on an `approved` story before the flip (`approved → cancelled`, as today). New: that path must refund the cap when `cap_consumed_on IS NOT NULL` and destroy the transit asset when `transit_asset_ref` is set — today an approved story never carried either.

### D4 — Container gone: `24/2207006` at the publish call

In the publish call's `MetaError` branch: `exc.code == 24` (Meta's "requested resource does not exist"; subcode 2207006 when given) → `_retry_or_poison(CONTAINER_GONE_RETRY_SECONDS, attempt=container_gone, step_back_to="transit_uploaded", wait=True, …)`. The permit record carries `container_gone: true` beside `meta`.

### D5 — Posted clears the error

Both posted transactions (real and dry run) set `last_error = NULL`.

### D6 — The final notice names the duration

`_retry_or_poison`'s poison branch computes the float's length from the first `container_create` permit of the current float (or the intent's `entered_state_at` of the first `publishing` hop) and says it: "A story couldn't be posted after 41 minutes of trying and needs attention: choose on its card, or open the Queue on the web."

## What is deliberately out of scope

- Serving the frame from our own API (F1). The float makes the refusals invisible; F1 removes the CDN from Meta's path and would also let us see Meta's fetches. Measured first: if refusals persist after the float, F1 is the next PR.
- Raising the worker's bulk concurrency (two tasks fleet-wide) — a throughput knob, unrelated to correctness.
- Error 9 holding the slot (above).

## Test plan (written first, watched red)

Gate (`tests/scripts/test_l5_pipeline_gate.py`, `TestTheFirstFetch` and a new `TestTheFloat`):

- A refused fetch is retried at once with a fresh url and posts in one run: two container calls, different urls, the second carrying the variant; the job's attempts unchanged; both permits recorded (`url_variant` 0 refused, 1 accepted); `last_error` NULL after posted.
- Four refusals in one run exhaust the fresh urls and step back: the story is `approved` with `publish_step = transit_uploaded`, the debit kept, the job `ready` at +30 s with its attempt restored, `attempts_by_step` = `{fetch_refusals: 4, url_variant: 3, fetch_waits: 1}`, the audit row `publishing → approved` with `detail.wait = "fetch"`.
- A stepped-back story re-enters through the flip without a second debit and continues at its step: one `daily_post_counts` increment across both runs; no second upload; posts.
- A sibling flips while the story waits (the slot is free): two intents on one account, the first waiting, the second posts; then the first re-enters and posts.
- The fetch ladder is indexed by fetch waits, not job attempts: a job with attempts = 3 and `fetch_waits = 1` gets the 60 s rung.
- The sixth fetch wait parks the story for review with the notice naming the minutes; cap retained, no lock (the existing poison assertions).
- A container Instagram cannot find is recreated, not re-published: `publish_outcomes=["container_gone"]` → `approved`, `publish_step = transit_uploaded`, `ig_container_id` NULL, job at +10 s; the next run creates a second container and posts; `publish_calls[1].container_id != publish_calls[0].container_id`.
- Cancel while floating refunds the debit and destroys the transit asset.
- A lost publish answer still parks ambiguous and never floats (existing test kept).
- K3/K4 kill tests unchanged (the checkpoints are the same).

Units: `test_transit.py` — `delivery_url(variant=)` appends the step and signs; variant 0 unchanged; variant 4 refused; `ready(variant=)` probes the variant url. `test_publish_cap` — the re-entrant flip (debit once; flip succeeds with an existing debit; key 4 still refuses a sibling). Mutation checks, one per test, as for #1302.

## What NOT to do

- Do not encode the wait in a column beside the state; the state machine already owns who holds the slot (owner, 2026-09-14).
- Do not let a fetch wait spend the job's attempts; the float's bound is its own ladder.
- Do not retry a lost publish answer; do not re-publish a container Instagram cannot find.
- Do not notify during a float; the card reads Approved until it reads Posted.
- Do not edit inside the `02` §4 pinned SQL fence; amend in prose after it (the advertised-DDL ratchet).
