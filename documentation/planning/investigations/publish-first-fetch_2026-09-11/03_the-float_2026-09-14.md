## Summary

Instagram refuses a freshly uploaded frame's first fetch about a quarter of the time, remembers the refusal per url for a minute or two, and once answered "resource does not exist" about a container it had reported ready four seconds earlier (`02_the-fetch-path_2026-09-13.md`). None of it is the file, the url type, the hour or our region, and all of it clears within minutes. The target state, ruled by the owner on 2026-09-14: **an approved story floats** — it tries, steps back, waits progressively longer, tries again, and the workspace sees "Approved" until it sees "Posted". A floating story holds nothing, so any number of them sit side by side and a burst drains at the speed of the successes. The workspace hears only if Instagram has refused for about half an hour straight.

Read with `00_INVESTIGATION.md`, `01_first-fetch-and-failure-reporting.md`, and `02_the-fetch-path_2026-09-13.md`.

## Target behaviour

For one approved story:

1. **Try.** Upload the frame, read its first bytes back (the readiness probe), ask Instagram for a container.
2. **Refused fetch → a fresh url at once.** Ask again within seconds with a url Instagram has never seen for this story — a separately derived, byte-identical frame — up to three fresh urls per round. Five of six such retries were accepted immediately in the experiments. (R) Fresh means fresh every round: a url refused thirty seconds ago is never offered again.
3. **Still refused → step back and wait.** Leave the account's publish slot, keep the progress and the cap debit, wait 30 s, then 60, 120, 300, 600, 900, and try a new round after each wait. Six waits: 33.5 minutes of trying in seven rounds.
4. **Container Instagram cannot find → recreate it.** On a publish-time `24/2207006`, drop the container id, step back, wait 10 s, then 30, 60, and create a new container from the same upload. Never re-publish an id Instagram says it cannot find.
5. **(R) Every wait steps back.** Not only the fetch and container ladders: Meta's rate-limit answers (codes 4/17), a lost container answer, a container still `pending` at the end of its poll budget, and the publish cap (Meta's error 9, or the local cap at the flip) all leave the slot while they wait. The rule has no exceptions: **a story never waits inside the slot.** Today every one of those holds it, and twenty floating siblings would poll it every 20 s for an hour.
   **(UX) A wait the user should know about is said on the card.** A cap wait is hours and changes what they would do next, so the card's line becomes "✅ Approved · posts tomorrow 09:00" (the next product slot, in the workspace's zone). A fetch or container wait is minutes and says nothing. The rule: tell the user about waits that matter, never about waits that don't.
6. **Posted.** Clear the story's error; the card reads "✅ Posted".
7. **Only then, the review card.** If the fetch ladder is spent (six waits) or the container ladder is spent (three), the story parks `review_required`: the card gains "👀 Needs review" and its three resolutions, and the workspace's channel gets one notice that says how long Instagram was tried ("Instagram refused this story for 34 minutes…"). "Post again" starts a fresh float, clean (R, UX): fresh counters, a fresh upload and url, no old container.
   **(UX) An approval is never silently undone.** The only exits from "Approved" are "Posted" and "Needs review". A dead job and the reaper's safety net (below) end on the same card with the same three buttons and a one-line notice that says what is known ("a fault on our side, five times"; "we lost track of this story") — never a story that reads Approved forever, never a quiet expiry of a decision the workspace made. Expiry stays what it is today: a card nobody answered.

What the workspace sees during 1–6: the card's "✅ Approved" line, nothing else. No notice for a retry in progress (already the rule).

Many at once: a waiting story is an `approved` row with its job due in the future. It holds no slot. Twenty stories float side by side; each steps back into the slot when its wait ends, and if a sibling is mid-call at that moment it re-checks after a short, growing pause (R: 20, 40, 60 s, never faster; today's flat 20 s is what would turn N floating siblings into 4,000 claims a day each behind a long hold).

## What does not float — unchanged

- **A lost publish answer.** Publishing is the irreversible step; the reconciler asks Instagram what happened before anything moves (`02` §6, R8). Never retried blind.
- **A definitive rejection of the file** (a terminal code other than 9004): fails and refunds, as today.
- **A workspace pause, a dry run**: as today. (R) A cancel is honoured at the next re-entry, with the refund and the destroy the story now carries — see D3.

## Design

### D1 — Fresh urls, fresh every round: `transit.delivery_url(ref, media_kind=, variant=n)`

Variant `n ≥ 1` appends `n` chained no-op steps (`dpr_1.0`, validated in the experiments) to the story chain: a distinct signed url and a distinct derived asset with identical bytes for every `n`; variant 0 is today's url. (R) The variant is **monotonic per story**: `n = attempts_by_step.fetch_refusals` at the time of the call, so each refusal advances the url and no round re-offers a refused one; a round tries at most three fresh urls. Bounded by the fetch ladder: each round's first url is the next variant too, so ≤ 4 × 7 = 28 urls (variants 0–27), 27 derivations per story in the worst case, against D38's credit trigger (revisit if the credit line moves). **(R, review of the PR) Images only:** a fresh url for a VIDEO is a fresh encode — tens of seconds, not the probe's budget — so a refused video offers no fresh urls; it steps back at once and offers the same url after the wait (accepted at +30 s in 10 of 17 trials, `02`). Eager variants for video are a follow-up if the ledger shows refused videos waiting. `ready()` takes `variant=` and probes the variant url (a first request derives it on the fly, 2–3 s, inside the probe's budget). Derived variants die with the original at destroy time (FC-3.5/3.6 unchanged). (R) A run is now up to four probe-and-call pairs, ≈ 2 minutes against the bulk lease's 120 s; the lease heartbeat covers it, as it covers the container poll today.

### D2 — Counters per failure class on the story: `post_intents.attempts_by_step`

The column exists (`{"v": 1}`, unused). It becomes `{"v": 1, "fetch_refusals": n, "fetch_waits": w, "container_gone": m, "float_since": "<iso>"}`, loaded onto the pipeline context, bumped in the SAME transaction as the permit resolution that records the failure (never a second transaction). Ladders index by the class's own count, never by the job's cumulative attempts:

| Class | Immediate retries | Waits (s) | Spent ⇒ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Fetch refused (`9004/2207052`) | fresh url, ×3 per round | 30, 60, 120, 300, 600, 900 | review card after the sixth wait |
| Container gone (`24/2207006`) | — | 10, 30, 60 (recreate) | review card after the third |
| Other retryable Meta answers, lost container answer, `pending` poll | — | 60, 300, 900, 3600 (unchanged) | review card (job attempts, unchanged) |
| Meta error 9 (publish cap) | — | the next product slot (unchanged) | — |

A fetch or container wait reschedules the job with the attempt RESTORED (`reschedule_job(restore_attempt=True)`): the job's five attempts stay the bound for the other classes and for crashes; the float is bounded by its own ladders, and the counters are bumped atomically with the reschedule, so nothing is unbounded (R: checked against `jobs.budget_exhausted`, `NO_DEADLINE`, the reaper's re-ready). `_retry_or_poison` gains `attempt=` (which count indexes the rung), `spent=` (that class's ladder is exhausted — what poisons), and `wait=` (step back). `float_since` is stamped at the first wait of a float and read by the notice (R: provider-op generations continue across "post again", so "the first permit of the float" is otherwise undefined).

(R) `resolve_review`'s "post again" (`command_executors.py`, `publish_cap.resolve_retry`) today copies `attempts_by_step` and bumps `retries`; it must ZERO `fetch_refusals`, `fetch_waits`, `container_gone` and clear `float_since` — otherwise a story parked at `fetch_waits: 6` re-parks on its first refusal and the notice reports a float that never ran.

### D3 — Step back: the new legal edge `publishing → approved`

- **The edges.** (R) Migration `076_publish_wait_edges.sql`: `INSERT INTO post_intent_transitions (from_state, to_state) VALUES ('publishing', 'approved'), ('approved', 'review_required') ON CONFLICT DO NOTHING`, with a `runner:postcondition` that both rows exist. The second edge is how a decision that the system could not carry out reaches the workspace's hands without a detour through `publishing` (UX principle 2). Because the ratchet requires the target lineage to be an ordered prefix of the advertised stream, the statement is a normative ```sql block appended to `07-security-model.md` as **§22** (the 073–075 shape), with a row in `scripts/advertised_ddl_manifest.json` (count 33 → 34 in `test_advertised_ddl.py`) and the file appended to the lineage list in `test_lineage_lane.py`. `02` §4's matrix table (prose, not fenced) gains the row and three amended guards: `approved → publishing` "or the debit it already carries"; `publishing → review_required` "or a class ladder spent"; `approved → cancelled|expired` "refund the debit and destroy the asset the story carries". `intent_ledger.py`'s header and migration 055's "27 edges" become 29 (`test_the_matrix_is_big_enough` asserts ≥ 27; nothing pins the count).
- **The hop.** On any wait, `_retry_or_poison` runs `UPDATE post_intents SET state = 'approved' WHERE id = :intent AND state = 'publishing'` in the same transaction as the permit resolution, the counter bump and the reschedule. `publish_step`, `transit_asset_ref`, `ig_container_id` (unless the container-gone rewind) and `cap_consumed_on` are untouched. (R) The guard trigger restamps `entered_state_at` on every hop and `trg_intent_audit` writes `detail NULL`, so the wait's detail rides a **direct audit row** in `_audit_deferral`'s shape (`event: "float_wait"`, the class, the rung, the counters) — two audit rows per hop, the trigger's and ours — and the duration comes from `float_since`, never from `entered_state_at`.
- **Re-entry: the flip made re-entrant.** (R, both lenses) `publish_cap.flip_to_publishing` becomes, as it will ship:

  ```
  WITH me AS (SELECT cap_consumed_on, cancel_requested FROM post_intents
               WHERE id = :intent AND state = 'approved'),
  debit AS (INSERT INTO daily_post_counts AS d (workspace_id, ig_account_id, local_date, count, cap_at_write)
            SELECT :ws, :acct, :local_date, 1, :cap FROM me
             WHERE me.cap_consumed_on IS NULL AND NOT me.cancel_requested
            ON CONFLICT (workspace_id, ig_account_id, local_date)
              DO UPDATE SET count = d.count + 1 WHERE d.count < d.cap_at_write
            RETURNING local_date),
  flip AS (UPDATE post_intents p SET state = 'publishing',
             cap_consumed_on = COALESCE(p.cap_consumed_on, (SELECT local_date FROM debit))
            WHERE p.id = :intent AND p.state = 'approved' AND NOT p.cancel_requested
              AND (p.cap_consumed_on IS NOT NULL OR EXISTS (SELECT 1 FROM debit))
            RETURNING id)
  SELECT (SELECT count(*) FROM debit) AS debited, (SELECT count(*) FROM flip) AS flipped,
         (SELECT count(*) FROM me) AS was_approved, (SELECT bool_or(cancel_requested) FROM me) AS cancelling;
  ```

  The tuple's contract, written down: `(1,1,1)` proceed with a fresh debit; `(0,1,1)` proceed, re-entry on the debit the story carries; `(0,0,1,false)` the cap denied it — defer to the next slot as today; `(0,0,1,true)` a cancel arrived — the cancel path; `(0,0,0)` the story is no longer approved — `IntentNotApproved`; `(1,0,·)` a debit without a flip — raise, as today. `COALESCE` keeps `ck_publishing_debited` true (the naive form would write NULL and raise a check violation that escapes the key-4 handling). Key 4 is still acquired by the flip, so a waiting story stepping back in while a sibling is mid-call gets `BUSY` (#1301) and re-checks after 20, 40, 60 s. (R) `test_a_non_approved_intent_raises…` in `test_publish_cap_gate.py`, which seeds a debited `publishing` row, is re-derived against the new tuple.
- **The cancel race, closed.** (R) `AND NOT cancel_requested` in the flip's own WHERE, so a cancel that lands between `_load`'s snapshot and the flip can never post after the card said cancelled — one window today, one per re-entry with the float. (R, review of the PR) When the flag lands inside the flip's own window the statement answers `(1,0)`: the debit rolls back and the pipeline's handler, finding the row `approved` and flagged, takes the cancel route (refund, `cancelled`, destroy) rather than a crash rung. The cancel path for a story that carries a debit and an asset: refund inside the transaction BEFORE the flip to `cancelled` (the terminal freeze forbids a refund after), destroy the transit asset after commit, never a provider call inside `_leased_tx`.
- **The ladder resumes from the stored `publish_step`** after the flip (`run_publish_pipeline` already does), so a re-entered story continues at `transit_uploaded` or wherever it left off. (R, review of the PR) A story that waited past the transit sweep (48 h: a weekend without slots, a long pause) re-enters to a frame the CDN no longer knows; a 404 after the probe's whole budget is taken as the asset gone — the story steps back to step `none` without its ref and the next run uploads afresh, on the job's ladder — never a review card for a frame nobody could have fetched. Any other not-ready answer keeps the asset and the step. And the pause is no exception to rule 5: a pause landing at the post-flip hold steps the story back too. (R) The usage pre-check, when armed, is skipped on re-entry (`cap_consumed_on IS NOT NULL`): a DEFER there would send a debited story with a swept asset to the next slot. A container-gone wait rewinds `publish_step` to `transit_uploaded` and nulls `ig_container_id` (the existing `step_back_to` path).
- **The two other exits of a debited `approved` row, owned — by the review card (R, both lenses; UX principle 2).**
  - *A dead job.* Five untyped crashes end a `publish_pipeline` job `failed` and it is never re-minted (`work_loop.py`), which today strands the story. New: the runner's exhausted path, for this kind, parks the story for review through the existing poison machinery — `publishing → review_required` when the crash left it mid-ladder, `approved → review_required` (the new edge) when it left it waiting — with the card restated ("👀 Needs review") and one notice: "We hit a fault on our side five times trying to post this story: choose on its card, or open the Queue on the web." The debit is retained, as review semantics already say (only "give up" refunds); the asset is kept for "post again".
  - *The reaper.* `fn_reap_expired`'s `approved → expired` leg (migration 059, a SECURITY DEFINER door) today expires an `approved` row after `approved_ttl_seconds` (72 h) — a silent undoing of a decision. New door version (`07` §22 material, with 076): an `approved` row past the TTL parks `review_required` instead, with the card restated and one notice ("We lost track of this story for three days: choose on its card…"). Nothing is refunded there; the review card's own rules apply. **(R, both lenses of the PR) The leg lists through a door:** the reap job is a system singleton (`app.tenant_id = ''`), under which a plain SELECT matches nothing once the worker runs as `svc_worker` — so `fn_reaper_stale_approved` (SECURITY DEFINER, the fourteenth `02` §7 door, in 076) lists the stale rows across tenants and the executor asserts each row's tenant before it parks; proven in the gate as the role with an empty tenant. A paused workspace's stories wait on purpose and are not listed. A stale row the workspace had flagged `cancel_requested` has no job left to honour the flag: the reaper refunds the debit it carries and cancels it, rather than park a story the workspace gave up on. `05` row 64's `approved_ttl` becomes "how long an approved story may go unfinished before the workspace is asked", and `06` §5 gains the row. The `approved → expired` edge stays seeded but unused by the reaper (removing an edge is a bigger change than not taking it; named so nobody "fixes" it back).
  - *"Post again", clean.* `resolve_review`'s retry starts a fresh float: counters zeroed, `float_since` cleared, `publish_step = 'none'`, `transit_asset_ref` and `ig_container_id` NULL (the old asset goes to the FC-3.6 sweep). A fresh upload is cheap and hands Meta a url it has never seen, which is the one thing the evidence says works.
- **Midnight.** (R) A story that re-enters on day D+1 keeps `cap_consumed_on = D`: the refund stays symmetric (it credits D's row), and D+1's count omits the story — the same shape as error 9 today. Stated in `02` §8 prose, not changed.

### D4 — Container gone: `24/2207006` at the publish call

In the publish call's `MetaError` branch: `exc.code == 24` → `_retry_or_poison(CONTAINER_GONE_RETRY_SECONDS, attempt=container_gone, step_back_to="transit_uploaded", wait=True, …)`. The permit record carries `container_gone: true` beside `meta`.

### D5 — Permits in the in-run loop

(R) `ctx.next_generation` reads `ctx.ops` loaded once; the second fresh url in a run must see the first's permit or it reuses the generation and trips `uq_ops_business_key`. Each permit and its resolution is appended to `ctx.ops` in the loop. `_permit_record` gains `url_variant`. A kill between a variant's permit and its call resumes exactly as K3 today: the resume marks the orphaned permit `lost_response`, bumps the generation, and the stored `fetch_refusals` decides the next url — the checkpoint order (probe → permit → call → resolve) is unchanged, so K3/K4 hold.

### D6 — Posted clears the error; the card and the notice say what is worth saying

`last_error = NULL` rides the `posted` UPDATE itself, both real and dry run (the terminal freeze forbids a later write). The poison notice reads `float_since`: "A story couldn't be posted after 34 minutes of trying and needs attention: choose on its card, or open the Queue on the web." A cap wait restates the card's line as "✅ Approved · posts tomorrow 09:00" through the existing restate-by-reference path (`outbox.restate_cards`), the time being the next product slot in the workspace's zone; when the story later posts, the line becomes "✅ Posted" as today. A fetch or container wait restates nothing. (R, review of the PR) The line is said only for a SLOT: when the clock has stamped none the deferral re-checks in a minute and says nothing (twenty deferred stories would otherwise edit their cards every minute). The local cap's line promises **tomorrow** without a time when the slot is later today — the day's count is at its cap, so that slot cannot post and the clock has not chosen tomorrow's time yet; Meta's error 9 and the usage pre-check's advisory keep the slot's time (their limit is not the local day's). `float_since` is stamped by the classes that TRY Instagram (fetch, container), not by a cap wait: the notice names the minutes Instagram was tried, and a wait for a slot is not a try. Counters are merged onto the row (`||`), never written wholesale from the pipeline's copy.

## What NOT to do

- Do not encode the wait in a column beside the state; the state machine already owns who holds the slot (owner, 2026-09-14).
- Do not let a fetch wait spend the job's attempts; the float's bound is its own ladder.
- Do not retry a lost publish answer; do not re-publish a container Instagram cannot find; do not re-offer a refused url.
- Do not notify during a float; the card reads Approved until it reads Posted.
- Do not edit inside the `02` §4 or `07` pinned SQL fences; amend in prose after them, and add new DDL only as a new `07` block with a manifest row (the advertised-DDL ratchet).
- Do not leave any exit from "Approved" but "Posted" and "Needs review": no story reads Approved forever, and no decision the workspace made is expired quietly. A cancel refunds; a review card retains until "give up".
- Do not tell the user about a wait of minutes; do tell them about a wait of hours, on the card.
